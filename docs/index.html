Where the seven days go

Rupeeflo's published onboarding flow and brokerage, taken apart

Rupeeflo publishes its whole account-opening flow, its step timings and both brokerage schedules. That is unusual, and it means three product questions can be answered from the outside, with arithmetic rather than opinion.

Both schedules are “0.5% or a flat cap, whichever is lower” — ₹200 on NRE, ₹50 on NRO. Below ₹10,000 a trade the percentage binds for both and the two accounts cost exactly the same. The gap opens between ₹10,000 and ₹40,000 and then stops growing forever at 4×. A customer investing ₹25,000 a month pays ₹900 a year more on NRE — against 31.2% TDS on NRO interest, which is the number that actually moves money. A comparison table that leads with brokerage is leading with the least important row.

Of six representative profiles, 3 of 6 are settled by a hard constraint before preference enters: conversion is NRO-only, derivatives are NRO-only, and a bank outside the PIS list closes the NRE route entirely. The customer already knows the answer to all of these. 4 questions resolve every branch, including the one case a wizard must refuse to answer — Indian income plus a repatriation need above the $1M NRO ceiling needs both accounts, and a human.

3. Seven days, fifty-five minutes

StepElapsedCustomer effort Appointment?Published source
Upload documents120 min—page: six documents, verification about one working day
Notary video call130 minyespage: 15-minute call, notarisation about another 15 minutes
Courier to Zerodha55 min—page: documents reach Zerodha in about five days

The published step timings reconcile exactly with the published 7-day headline, which is worth checking before trusting either. The customer spends 55 minutes in total; 99.5% of the elapsed time is waiting, and 71% is a courier moving paper after the customer has already finished. So the lever on “it takes a week” is mostly communication, not speed. Exactly one step — the notary call — needs the customer to meet another person at a set time, and that is the only step that can fail on calendars rather than on effort.

4. And three good steps still lose a quarter of the cohort

StepEnteringSurviving Lost
Upload documents100%90.0%10.0 pp
Notary video call90%81.0%9.0 pp
Courier to Zerodha81%72.9%8.1 pp

Every step at a healthy 90% still ends at 72.9% end to end — sequential completion compounds, and three steps at 90% is not 90%. At equal rates the earliest step costs the most customers in absolute terms, which is an argument for moving whatever can be moved later in the flow. These rates are an assumption, not a measurement — no drop-off data is published, and none is invented here. What the model supplies is the arithmetic; the rates would come from Rupeeflo's own funnel.

What I would want before proposing anything

Real completion rates per step, and the distribution of customer trade sizes — the first decides which step to attack, the second decides whether the brokerage row deserves its prominence at all. Also where the notary call gets rescheduled, since that is the only step with a coordination failure mode, and whether the courier step can report progress, since that is where most of the week goes.
